Part 0

Every workload routed, no line yet priced

Sessions 1 through 4 built the routing. Tonight builds the budget. The difference matters because a routing decision that cannot state its true monthly cost is a decision waiting to be reversed by whoever finds the hidden line items first - usually finance, usually in a bad mood.

★ What you walk out with tonight. Per-token arithmetic you can do on a napkin with real rates. The four-layer true-cost stack that explains how a $5 API bill became a $2,315 monthly loss. And your own hand-priced total for the whole Cadence backlog, reconciled against the simulator's canon.

LiveThe bill is not the cost3 min▶

The simulator's baseline configuration has an API bill of five dollars. Its true monthly cost is $2,315. Nothing in that gap is exotic: it is seven silently wrong numbers at $330 of analyst cleanup each. The most expensive line in an AI budget has never once appeared on an AI invoice.

Tonight's discipline is simple to state: price the whole stack, not the meter. The meter reads tokens. The stack includes the eval set someone maintains, the monitoring someone watches, the checking someone does, and the cleanup someone gets pulled into. A route is not cheap because its meter reads low. It is cheap when the whole stack reads low - and the deterministic tier keeps winning precisely because its stack is almost all zeros.

Self-studyWhy honest numbers go up before they go down2 min▶

Watch the simulator's arc across the sessions: $2,315 at baseline, $225 after triage, $224 on the free tier, $62 with classic ML, $176 with GenAI fit - and then $416 when full costing arrives. The last step goes UP, and it is the most trustworthy number in the whole table, because it is the first one carrying evals, monitoring, and upkeep on every probabilistic route.

A budget that only ever goes down is usually a budget that stopped counting something. The routed config's $416 against the baseline's $2,315 is still better than 5x cheaper - with 10 out of 10 answers trusted instead of 0. That is the two-number story worth telling: the honest number is bigger than the bill, and dramatically smaller than the chaos it replaced.

Part 2 · covers the true-cost stack

The API bill is the smallest layer

6 min live

Token math prices the meter. The true cost of a probabilistic route stacks three more layers on top of it, and the baseline's $2,315 came almost entirely from the layer nobody budgets.

LiveThe four layers, priced4 min▶

Every probabilistic route carries the full stack. Every deterministic route skips almost all of it:

  • Layer 1 · The bill. Tokens and queries, from Part 1's rate card. The only layer with an invoice.
  • Layer 2 · Evals and monitoring: $40 per probabilistic workload per month. An eval set someone maintains and a dashboard someone watches. A probabilistic route with no evals budgeted is a route that has not been approved - it has just not failed loudly yet. Cadence's six probabilistic workloads carry 6 x $40 = $240, which is exactly the gap between the $176 bill and the $416 true cost.
  • Layer 3 · Verification labor: $220 a month whenever checkable answers flow through an LLM. If a human must re-derive the number to trust it, the route is paying twice for one answer - which is the accounting argument for never sending checkable work there in the first place.
  • Layer 4 · Cleanup: $330 per silently wrong number - six analyst-hours of finding it, tracing it, and repairing what it touched. Seven of these made the baseline $2,315 while its bill read $5.

Error-cost asymmetry is the risk half of the math. A wrong ticket theme wastes minutes and self-corrects at the next spot-check. A wrong revenue number reaches a decision, and the decision does not reconcile itself. Price the blast radius of a miss, not just the frequency - it is why the human gate from session 4 sits on emails and not on theme labels.

Self-studyThe trillion-dollar version of the same question4 min▶

The stack scales up. Goldman Sachs' 2024 report "Gen AI: Too Much Spend, Too Little Benefit?" is the same accounting argument at macro size, and it is a genuine debate inside one document. Jim Covello, the skeptic, argues that roughly $1 trillion of AI capex has "little to show for it so far" and that AI is not designed to solve the complex problems that would justify the costs. Daron Acemoglu, in the same issue, estimates under 5 percent of tasks profitably automatable and around 0.9 percent GDP impact over a decade.

And in the SAME report, Goldman's own analysts - Briggs, Rangan, and Sheridan - argue the bull side. Quote it as a debate or do not quote it at all: a board paper citing only Covello is as unbalanced as one citing only the bulls. The lesson a router takes from it is neither camp's headline: the spend is real, the returns are concentrated, and honest stack-level accounting is how you land on the right side of the concentration. Cadence's $416 against $2,315 is the same story with fewer zeros.

Part 3 · covers the energy correction

The energy number, corrected - and why the correction is the lesson

5 min live

One more cost line circulates in every AI debate: energy per query. The number most people quote is stale, the measured number is far smaller, and knowing which is which is the same epistemic skill this whole course trains.

LiveWhat the measured numbers say3 min▶

The figure everyone has heard - 2.9 Wh per ChatGPT query, ten times a Google search - traces to 2024-era estimation (EPRI lineage), built before anyone with production telemetry had published. It has since been superseded by measurement. Google's August 2025 study of live production traffic puts the median Gemini text prompt at 0.24 Wh, 0.03 grams of CO2e, and 0.26 milliliters of water - and reports per-prompt energy down 33x in twelve months. Epoch AI's independent estimate lands in the same neighborhood: a typical GPT-4o query around 0.3 Wh.

Two honest qualifiers travel with the good news. These are medians for text prompts - median is not mean, and agentic or long-context workloads run far heavier than the typical chat turn. And the 33x trend means any number, alarming or reassuring, goes stale fast: quote it with its date or not at all.

Self-studyThe correction is the transferable skill3 min▶

Here is why an energy footnote earns a section in a costing session: quoting the stale 2.9 Wh figure in a board paper is the same failure as quoting "95 percent of pilots fail" without its learning-gap caveat. Both numbers were once defensible. Both acquired better evidence. Both keep circulating because they are vivid, and a leader who repeats either one unrefreshed has told the room something false while citing a real source.

The hygiene that fixes both is identical: prefer measurement over estimation when measurement exists, carry the date on every number, and carry the distributional caveat - median is not mean, a pilot statistic is not a capability statistic. A routing playbook is only as credible as the sloppiest number quoted in its defense.

Real world

Cadence's sustainability-minded board member arrived with the 10x-a-Google-search line and a proposal to freeze all GenAI spend. The data lead did not argue values - she updated the number: measured medians, dated, caveats attached, next to the true-cost stack showing exactly which three workloads earn their tokens. The freeze became a monitoring line item. Better numbers, calmly delivered, are the whole job.

Build-along

Price the routes, by hand

★ 20 min · everyone builds

Napkins out. Every number you need is on this page: the rate card, the stack layers, and the simulator canon to reconcile against. If your hand total and the canon disagree, one of you has forgotten a layer - and it is usually not the simulator.

Price transcript extraction on three engines. 660 runs x (9,000 in + 500 out) tokens. On Sonnet 5 ($2/$10) that is about $15 of raw tokens; on Haiku 4.5 ($1/$5) about $7.60; on gpt-5-mini ($0.25/$2) about $2.10. That spread IS session 4's cascade in dollars - and the question "which tier is capable enough" is now worth a precise answer, because you know what the answer costs.

Add the upkeep layer and re-total. Every probabilistic route carries $40 a month of eval and monitoring upkeep - no exceptions, or the route is not approved. Cadence has six probabilistic workloads: add $240 to whatever your token math said. Notice how upkeep dwarfs the token bill on the small lines: the extraction route's real price is upkeep-dominated at any tier.

Price one silent miss against a year of the query. One silently wrong revenue number costs $330 of cleanup - six analyst-hours. The BigQuery query that gets the same number right runs inside the free TiB, effectively $0 per run, on a deterministic tier whose whole bill line is about $4 a month. One incident outcosts months of the tier that never has incidents. Write that ratio down; it is the sharpest sentence in your budget defense.

Reconcile against the canon. Bill $176 for the routed config, plus 6 x $40 upkeep = $416 true cost, 10/10 trusted, zero silently wrong. If you landed above, you double-counted verification - the routed config sends no checkable answers through an LLM, so the $220 verify line is $0 by design. If you landed below, find the probabilistic workload you forgot to charge upkeep on.

Write the one-line budget defense. "The honest number is bigger and it is the one that survives an audit." Then put both numbers under it: $416 fully costed against the baseline's $2,315 fully costed - better than 5x cheaper, with every answer trusted. A bill-only comparison would have looked even more flattering, and it would have deserved exactly the scrutiny it got.

Keep the napkin. Session 6 runs the entire backlog live in the simulator, lever by lever, and your hand-priced totals are the check against the machine. A router who can reproduce the number on paper is a router nobody can bluff.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · A workload runs 100 times a month at 10,000 tokens in and 1,000 tokens out per run, on Claude Sonnet 5 ($2 in / $10 out per 1M tokens, as of Sept 2026). Raw token cost?

100 x 10,000 = 1M input tokens = $2. 100 x 1,000 = 0.1M output tokens at $10/M = $1. Total $3. Input and output are priced separately - never blend them - and the true cost adds $40 of eval and monitoring upkeep on top, which at this volume is thirteen times the token bill.

2 · The baseline configuration's true cost is $2,315 a month while its API bill is $5. Which layer created the gap?

7 x $330 = $2,310, plus the $5 bill, is the whole $2,315. The baseline pays almost nothing to the API and a fortune to the analysts who mop up after it. Upkeep is the honest routed config's story ($240 of the $416); the baseline never budgeted evals at all, which is exactly why it is mopping.

3 · A board paper says "each AI query burns 2.9 Wh, ten times a Google search." What is the correct 2026 response?

The 2.9 Wh figure (EPRI 2024 lineage) is superseded by measured production data, so repeating it unrefreshed is quoting a stale number from a real source - the same failure as "95% fail" without its caveat. But B keeps the caveats too: median is not mean, text prompts are not agentic runs, and every energy number needs its date attached.
